Premium number score

A transparent 0–100 score for how collectible a number is, with every point explained and an indicative value range.

What the score measures

Length Shorter is rarer. A 3-digit number starts with 32 points; a 10-digit number with 6.
Pattern Repdigits (8888) add the most, then round numbers, triples, sequences, double pairs (115522), repeating blocks and palindromes.
Simplicity Fewer distinct digits are easier to remember and sell.
Cultural digits Each 8 adds value; 6 and 9 add a little; each 4 subtracts; zeros subtract slightly.
Special codes 786, 108, 520 and 1314 carry cultural premiums.

For numeric .com domains the indicative range is anchored to public sales by length (for example, 6-digit .coms have sold from about $100 to $17,000 in recent years). It is a starting point, not an appraisal: the real price depends on the buyer pool that week. See record sales and our numeric domain guide.

Questions

Is this an official valuation?

No. It’s an indicative, rules-based score. For a sale, ask the concierge for an expert valuation.

Why does 4 lower the score?

Four sounds like “death” in Chinese, Japanese and Korean, and East Asian buyers are a large part of the premium-number market.

Why do six-digit domains vary so much?

Most random 6-digit .coms trade near wholesale; patterned ones like AABBCC or ABCABC attract collectors and sell for multiples.
