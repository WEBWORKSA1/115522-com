UAE number plate auctions: how to bid and what sells

Updated 8 October 20265 min readBy the 115522 editorial team

In the UAE, distinctive number plates are sold at government-run auctions in each emirate: Dubai's Roads and Transport Authority (RTA) holds regular online and open auctions, while Abu Dhabi, Sharjah and others sell plates through their transport or police authorities, often in partnership with Emirates Auction. Value comes from fewer digits, desirable letter codes and repeated or matching patterns. The record is Dubai's P7, sold for AED 55 million at a 2023 charity auction.

Who runs plate auctions

Emirate Authority Typical format
Dubai RTA Regular online auctions (week-long bidding) and periodic live "open" auctions at hotel venues
Abu Dhabi Integrated Transport Centre / Abu Dhabi Police, with Emirates Auction Live and online auctions of distinctive plates
Sharjah Sharjah Police, with Emirates Auction Public auctions of distinctive plates
Charity events Emirates Auction with government partners Occasional "Most Noble Numbers" style auctions where proceeds go to a named cause

Emirates Auction, a Dubai-based company, has been the operating partner for many of the region's headline plate sales, including the 2008 Abu Dhabi "1" sale and the 2023 P7 sale. Plates are also traded privately between owners, which is where much of the day-to-day market activity happens.

What makes a UAE plate valuable

  • Digit count. The single biggest factor. A one-digit plate sits in a different price universe from a two-digit plate, and each extra digit cuts value sharply. Five-digit plates are common and modest in price.
  • The code letter. Dubai plates carry a code (one or two letters such as A, P, AA, BB, CC). Older and single-letter codes tend to be prized, and buyers often want a code that pairs with the number, such as CC22 or BB20.
  • Repetition and patterns. All-same digits (777, 9999), matching code and number, and simple sequences command premiums.
  • Personal meaning. Birth years, national dates and favourite numbers drive bidding among individuals; companies sometimes chase numbers that match a brand or phone line.
  • Emirate. A one-digit plate in Dubai or Abu Dhabi is more visible and generally more valuable than an equivalent in a smaller emirate.

How to bid in a Dubai RTA auction

RTA publishes each auction in advance, listing the codes and number of plates on offer. The requirements are similar across its online and open auctions, though deposit amounts differ.

  1. Have a Dubai traffic file. RTA auctions are open to customers with a traffic file in Dubai. Residents get one when they register a vehicle or licence; companies have their own.
  2. Register during the registration window. Registration usually opens about a week before bidding, through the RTA website, the RTA app, or Customer Happiness Centres (Umm Ramool, Deira and Al Barsha).
  3. Pay the participation fee. RTA charges a non-refundable fee of AED 120.
  4. Provide the security deposit. For recent online auctions, bidders provide a security cheque of AED 5,000 payable to RTA. For the larger open auctions, the deposit has been AED 25,000.
  5. Bid. Online auctions run for a set period (recently seven days), with bidding through RTA's digital channels. Open auctions are live, held in a hotel ballroom with paddle bidding.
  6. Pay if you win. In recent online auctions, winners had 10 working days to pay. Amounts up to AED 50,000 could be paid in cash at authorised centres; larger sums by manager's cheque or credit card.

Bidding in Abu Dhabi and Sharjah

Abu Dhabi and Sharjah auctions are announced by the transport or police authority and are typically run by Emirates Auction, with in-person bidding and, for many sales, online participation as well. Bidders usually register in advance and lodge a refundable guarantee, often by cheque; unsuccessful bidders get it back. At the 2019 Sharjah auction, for example, starting bids ranged from AED 5,000 to AED 1 million depending on the plate, and winners could register the plate in their own name or transfer it to someone else after meeting registration requirements.

Transfer basics

A distinctive plate in the UAE is an asset that can outlive the car it sits on. In Dubai:

  • Plates can be transferred between people. The seller starts a plate ownership transfer through RTA's Dubai Drive app using UAE Pass, enters the buyer's details, signs the sale agreement digitally and pays a fee of AED 120. The buyer then receives a plate transfer certificate.
  • Plates can be moved between your own vehicles. Owners commonly keep a plate and attach it to a new car when they sell the old one.
  • Clear fines first. Outstanding traffic fines on either party's file can block transactions.

Plates registered in one emirate generally stay with that emirate's licensing system, so moving a plate from Abu Dhabi to Dubai is not a simple transfer. Confirm with both authorities before buying a plate from another emirate.

Buying privately: precautions

  1. Verify ownership through the official app or a Customer Happiness Centre before paying anything.
  2. Complete the transfer through RTA (or the relevant emirate authority) at the same time as payment, not afterwards.
  3. Be wary of very cheap "rare" plates offered online; plate scams usually rely on payment before an official transfer.
  4. Compare against recent auction results for similar digit counts and codes. Our vehicle number tool and premium score help you judge the pattern strength of a plate.

Questions

What is the most expensive number plate ever sold?

Dubai's P7 sold for AED 55 million at the Most Noble Numbers charity auction in April 2023, with proceeds going to the 1 Billion Meals Endowment. It beat Abu Dhabi's plate 1, which sold for AED 52.2 million in 2008.

What do I need to bid in a Dubai RTA auction?

You need a Dubai traffic file, a non-refundable AED 120 registration fee and a security cheque payable to RTA. Recent online auctions required AED 5,000, while the larger open auctions have required AED 25,000.

Is VAT charged on Dubai number plates?

Yes. RTA applies 5% VAT to its plate auction sales, so the total cost is higher than the hammer price.

How do I transfer a plate to another person in Dubai?

The seller applies through RTA's Dubai Drive app using UAE Pass, enters the buyer's details, signs the sale agreement digitally and pays an AED 120 fee. The buyer then receives a plate transfer certificate.
