Hong Kong personalised plates and number auctions

Updated 8 October 20265 min readBy the 115522 editorial team

Hong Kong sells vehicle registration marks through Transport Department auctions, and the proceeds, after expenses, go to the Government Lotteries Fund for charity. There are two families of marks: traditional marks (up to four digits, optionally with a one- or two-letter prefix) and personalised marks of up to eight characters, which you propose yourself. Personalised marks start at a HK$5,000 reserve, while single-letter and low-number traditional marks have sold for up to HK$26 million.

Traditional vs personalised marks

Traditional marks (TVRM) Personalised marks (PVRM)
Format Up to 4 digits, with an optional 1 or 2 letter prefix (for example 28, R, BB 2288) Up to 8 letters, numerals and spaces of your choosing (for example "10VE Y0U")
Introduced Auctions since 1973 Applications first accepted April 2006
How a mark comes to auction You apply for an available mark and it joins a waiting list, or the department offers special marks itself You submit a combination during an application period; approved marks go to auction
Deposit or fee to apply HK$1,000 refundable deposit for ordinary marks; none for special and HK/XX marks No payment on application; HK$5,000 deposit if your application is selected and passes initial checks
Reserve price Set per category and published for each auction HK$5,000
Bidding Ordinary marks on the E-Auction platform; special and HK/XX marks by paddle at live auctions Paddle bidding at live auctions
Transferable with the car? Ordinary marks yes; special marks no (allocation is cancelled if the vehicle changes owner) Assigned only to a vehicle registered in the purchaser's name

Rules for personalised combinations

The Transport Department's guidance sets out what a personalised mark may contain:

  • No more than 8 characters, counting letters, numerals and spaces.
  • The letters I, O and Q are not allowed (I is read as 1 and O as 0, which is why plates like "N0THANKS" use a zero).
  • At most one space between any two characters.
  • No more than four identical characters side by side.
  • In a two-row layout, a maximum of four characters per row.
  • No duplication of, or close resemblance to, existing marks, permit numbers or reserved marks.

Applications are invited in January, May and September, and each applicant may submit one application per exercise.

How the process works

Personalised marks

  1. Apply during an application period with your proposed combination. No money is due at this stage.
  2. Selection and deposit. If your application is drawn and meets the basic rules, you pay a HK$5,000 deposit.
  3. Vetting and approval. Approved marks are scheduled for auction and you are notified by post.
  4. Auction. The mark is offered with a HK$5,000 reserve. As the applicant you are expected to bid, including the opening bid at the reserve; otherwise someone else may win it at that price. If nobody bids, the mark can be allocated to you for a HK$5,000 special fee, covered by your deposit.
  5. Pay immediately. Winners complete a memorandum of sale and pay straight after bidding, by the Easy Pay System or a crossed cheque to the HKSAR Government. Cash and credit cards are not accepted.
  6. Assign within 12 months. You must apply to put the mark on a vehicle registered in your name within 12 months, or the allocation can be cancelled.

Traditional marks

  1. Check the mark is available through the department's online enquiry or hotline.
  2. Apply to reserve it. Ordinary marks need a HK$1,000 refundable deposit.
  3. Wait for an auction slot. The department warns of waits of 12 to 18 months or more for ordinary marks and 24 months or more for special and HK/XX marks.
  4. Bid online on E-Auction (ordinary marks) or by paddle at a live auction (special and HK/XX marks).
  5. If an ordinary mark you applied for draws no bids, it can be allocated to you for a HK$1,000 special fee.

Record sales

The Transport Department publishes its top-ten prices. The most valuable marks are single letters and lucky low numbers, nearly all sold at the Lunar New Year auction, when demand for auspicious marks peaks.

Why these marks? Single letters are the shortest possible marks and are often initials of a person or company. Among numbers, 8 is favoured because in Cantonese it sounds like prosperity, so 18 suggests "sure to prosper" and 28 "easy prosperity", while 9 is linked with long life. What personalised marks typically sell for

Most personalised marks sell for modest sums. At the February 2023 Lunar New Year auction, the top personalised mark on the first day was "10VE Y0U" at HK$310,000, followed by "1 RR" at HK$110,000, while "N0THANKS" went for HK$40,000. The bulk of personalised marks sell at or near the HK$5,000 reserve, because the applicant is often the only bidder. A personalised mark is mainly a form of self-expression; the high prices are concentrated in short traditional marks.

Where the money goes

The Transport Department states that auction proceeds, after operating expenses, are paid into the Government Lotteries Fund, which finances social welfare projects. This charitable link is part of why the Lunar New Year auction is a public event with press coverage of the top bids.

Tips for buyers

  • Budget for total cost. The hammer price is not the only expense; you still pay vehicle licensing and plate manufacture.
  • Know transferability. Special traditional marks cannot follow the car to a new owner, which affects resale strategy.
  • Keep the Certificate of Allocation. It is your proof of ownership of the mark and is needed if you transfer the vehicle.
  • Be precise. A personalised mark cannot be rearranged after the auction; spacing is fixed as approved.
  • Test meaning in both languages. A combination that reads well in English may sound unlucky in Cantonese. Our vehicle number tool flags common digit associations.

Questions

How many characters can a Hong Kong personalised plate have?

Up to eight letters, numerals and spaces. The letters I, O and Q are not allowed, and no more than four identical characters may sit side by side.

How much does a personalised mark cost?

Approved marks are auctioned with a HK$5,000 reserve price, and applicants pay a HK$5,000 deposit once selected. Most sell at or near the reserve, though popular phrases can fetch hundreds of thousands of dollars.

What is the most expensive plate sold in Hong Kong?

The single letter W sold for HK$26 million on 7 March 2021. R followed at HK$25.5 million in February 2023.

Where does the auction money go?

After operating expenses, proceeds go to the Government Lotteries Fund, which supports social welfare projects.
