Fancy vehicle numbers in India: e-auction guide

Updated 8 October 20266 min readBy the 115522 editorial team

In India, fancy or "choice" vehicle registration numbers are sold by state transport departments, mostly through online e-auctions on the central Parivahan fancy-number portal. You register, pay a category-based fee, bid during a short window, and pay the balance within a few days if you win. Reserve prices start from a few thousand rupees for ordinary choice numbers and reach ₹5 lakh for 0001 on a car in Delhi, while the national record stands at ₹1.17 crore for HR88B8888 in November 2025.

What counts as a fancy number

An Indian registration mark has four parts: the state code (for example DL, HR, KL), the RTO district code, a series of one to three letters, and a four-digit number from 0001 to 9999. The "fancy" part is almost always the four-digit number. States sort these into categories, with the scarcest at the top:

  • Single-digit style: 0001 to 0009, especially 0001.
  • All-same digits: 1111, 7777, 8888, 9999.
  • Round and patterned: 0100, 1000, 1234, 0786, 5050.
  • Any other out-of-sequence number: a number you choose that is simply not the next one in line.

Because the series letters can also be chosen when a new series opens, the most valuable plates combine a matching district code and series, as in HR88B8888, where the district code is 88 and the letter B visually resembles an 8.

Delhi reserve prices as an example

Every state sets its own categories and reserve prices. Delhi is the most frequently cited benchmark. Published guides based on the Delhi transport department's schedule list four-wheeler reserve prices along these lines:

Category Examples Reserve price (four-wheeler)
1 0001 ₹5,00,000
2 0002 to 0009 ₹3,00,000
3 0010 to 0099, 7777, 9999 and similar ₹2,00,000
4 0100, 0555, 8888 and similar ₹1,50,000
5 Other out-of-sequence numbers ₹25,000 (often first-come, first-served)

Two-wheeler reserve prices are much lower; Delhi's 0001 for a two-wheeler is commonly listed at ₹50,000. Schedules are revised from time to time, so always confirm the current figure for your RTO on the portal before budgeting.

The reserve price is only the starting point. Delhi's 0001 sold for ₹7.55 lakh in a 2015 e-auction round, and in that same round 0007 fetched ₹10.45 lakh, more than double its reserve.

The e-auction process, step by step

Most states now use the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways portal at fancy.parivahan.gov.in. The broad flow is the same everywhere, although timings vary by state.

  1. Create a public user account. Register with your name, mobile number and email, and verify both. Login uses an OTP.
  2. Select state and RTO. Pick the office where the vehicle will be registered. You can usually only bid for numbers in the series currently open at that RTO.
  3. Check availability. The portal lists numbers open for booking and their categories.
  4. Register for the number. Pay the registration or participation fee for that category. In many states this is non-refundable, while the security deposit portion may be adjusted against the final price or refunded if you lose.
  5. Bid during the bidding window. Bids rise in fixed increments set by category. If only one person registers for a number, it is typically allotted at the reserve price.
  6. Pay the balance if you win. Winners are notified by SMS and email and must pay the remaining amount online within the state's deadline, commonly a few working days.
  7. Download the allotment letter. Present it at the RTO when registering the vehicle, within the validity period stated on the letter (often around 90 days).

In Haryana, for example, fancy numbers run on a weekly cycle that closes on Wednesday. Bidders pay a ₹10,000 security deposit plus a ₹1,000 registration fee to take part.

The HR88B8888 sale is a useful lesson in itself. The reserve was only ₹50,000, the number drew 45 bidders, and the final bid of ₹1.17 crore was widely reported as an Indian record. The winning bidder then failed to pay by the deadline in early December 2025, forfeited the ₹10,000 deposit, and the transport department announced the number would be re-auctioned. A headline bid is not the same as a completed sale.

How states differ

  • Categories and prices. A number that is category 3 in one state may be category 2 in another. Some states price 0786 highly because of its religious significance; others treat it as an ordinary choice number.
  • Auction cadence. Some states run weekly cycles; others open numbers only when a new series starts at an RTO.
  • Retention. Several states let you retain an existing number when you sell your old vehicle and transfer it to a new one, for a fee. Delhi's published schedule prices retention as a percentage of the category's reserve with a minimum amount.
  • Two-wheelers. Reserve prices are a fraction of car prices, which makes bikes the cheapest entry point for collectors.
  • Portal coverage. A few states still use their own transport websites or offline counters for some categories, so check your state transport department's notices.

Timeline from booking to number plate

Stage Typical duration
Registration window for an auction About 2 to 3 days
Bidding window About 2 to 3 days
Balance payment after winning Usually within 5 days (varies by state)
Allotment letter validity Often around 90 days
RTO registration and HSRP plate Days to a few weeks

Plan the vehicle purchase so that the allotment letter does not expire before the dealer completes registration. Many buyers book the number first and time delivery of the car around it.

Tips before you bid

  1. Set a ceiling in advance. Auction excitement is real; the HR88B8888 episode shows how bids can outrun a bidder's ability to pay.
  2. Read the state's fee rules. Know which part of what you pay is refundable if you lose and what you forfeit if you win and do not pay.
  3. Bid on the right RTO. The number is tied to the RTO where you register the vehicle, and that office must match your address proof.
  4. Watch the last hours. Most competitive bidding happens close to the deadline.
  5. Consider less obvious numbers. Numbers matching your birth date or the digits you personally like often carry no premium at all. Our vehicle number analyser checks the digit total and patterns.
  6. Remember the plate itself is standardised. Fancy numbers must still be displayed on a high-security registration plate in the prescribed font; stylised lettering is not allowed.

Questions

How much does 0001 cost in Delhi?

Delhi's published reserve price for 0001 on a four-wheeler is ₹5 lakh, and the final price depends on bidding. For two-wheelers the 0001 reserve is commonly listed at ₹50,000.

What is the most expensive vehicle number sold in India?

HR88B8888 drew a winning bid of ₹1.17 crore in a Haryana e-auction in November 2025. The bidder did not pay by the deadline, so the deposit was forfeited and the number was put up for re-auction.

Where do I bid for a fancy number?

Most states use the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways portal at fancy.parivahan.gov.in. You register as a public user, choose your state and RTO, pay the fee for the number's category and bid during the auction window.

Is the registration fee refunded if I lose?

It depends on the state. Many states treat the registration or participation fee as non-refundable, while a separate security deposit may be refunded or adjusted, so read your state's rules before bidding.
