Numeric domain names: 3N to 6N .com value guide

Updated 8 October 20265 min readBy the 115522 editorial team

Numeric domain names are .com addresses made only of digits, grouped by length: 3N (three digits, such as 260.com), 4N, 5N and 6N. Short numerics are a scarce, liquid asset class driven largely by Chinese buyers, while 6N names are plentiful and mostly trade between investors at low four-figure prices or less. Value depends on length, digit choice (8, 6 and 9 help; 4 and often 0 hurt) and pattern.

The market by length

There are only 1,000 possible 3N .com names, 10,000 possible 4N names, 100,000 possible 5N names and 1,000,000 possible 6N names. Every one of the shorter sets has been registered for years, so all trading happens on the aftermarket. Supply grows tenfold with each extra digit, and prices fall accordingly.

Length Possible names Illustrative public sales Typical buyer
3N 1,000 360.com reported at about $17 million (2015, unconfirmed); 708.com $327,000 (2017); 147.com $56,555 (2021) Companies, large investors
4N 10,000 8181.com $225,000 (2015); 8499.com $82,000 (2021); 2698.com $18,656 (2021) Companies, investors
5N 100,000 88877.com $18,500 and 55777.com $16,250 (both March 2024) Investors, some end users
6N 1,000,000 666777.com $16,750; 221199.com $10,000; 339999.com $8,258 (all in the year to September 2021) Almost entirely investors

Those 6N figures are the outliers. A 2021 review of NameBio data found only 25 six-digit .com names that sold for more than $1,500 in a full year, which shows how thin the high end of that segment is.

Why Chinese buyers dominate

For many Chinese internet users, digits are easier to type and remember than English words, and a string of digits can be read aloud as a phrase. Each digit has a sound in Mandarin or Cantonese that resembles a word, so a numeric string can spell out a wish or a slogan. A 2021 Domain Name Wire analysis, for example, read 2698 as a phrase about a safe road to lasting wealth and 9599 as "wine house 99".

The core digit associations are:

  • 8: sounds like prosperity; the most valuable digit.
  • 6: smooth progress; popular in business.
  • 9: long-lasting; 99 and 98 read as lasting or lasting wealth.
  • 5: can sound like "I" or "me".
  • 4: sounds like death; it lowers value in almost any position.
  • 0: opinions differ. Many investors treat it as the second-least-wanted digit, especially at the start of a name, yet some high-value names contain it.

What drives value

  1. Length. Each digit removed multiplies scarcity by ten. Length outweighs nearly everything else.
  2. No 4, and usually no 0. Investors often label 6N names without 0 or 4 as "premium" or "Chinese premium". Removing those two digits leaves 262,144 six-digit combinations (8 choices for each of 6 positions), so even premium 6N names are far from rare.
  3. Repetition and structure. Patterns such as AAAAAA (888888), AAABBB (666777), AABBCC (221199), AABBBB (339999), ABABAB and ABCABC sell well above random strings.
  4. Lucky endings. Endings like 88, 888, 99, 168 ("prosperity all the way") or 518 (often read as "I will prosper") add appeal.
  5. Readable meaning. A string that reads as a phrase, a date (for example 520 or 1314 for love), or a company stock code can attract an end user.
  6. Extension. .com dominates. Some .cn and .net numerics trade, but at far lower prices.

How to appraise a numeric domain

Automated appraisals are unreliable for numerics because they are trained mostly on word domains. A better method:

  1. Classify the name. Note its length, whether it contains 0 or 4, and its pattern type.
  2. Pull comparable sales. Search NameBio for names with the same length and pattern, filtered to the last two or three years. Daily sales round-ups on NamePros and the weekly DNJournal charts also list notable numeric sales.
  3. Separate wholesale from retail. Auction results between investors (for example on GoDaddy auctions) represent the wholesale market. Prices paid by end users through brokers or marketplaces are retail and can be several times higher, but they happen less often.
  4. Check Chinese platforms. Asking prices and closed sales on Chinese marketplaces show what domestic buyers will pay, often quoted in yuan.
  5. Discount for time. A retail asking price is only meaningful alongside an estimate of how long it may take to sell.

Risks to understand

  • Liquidity. Short numerics can usually be sold quickly at wholesale. Most 6N names cannot; a 2021 study of China's top internet companies found that none used a six-digit or longer domain, so the end-user market for long numerics is tiny.
  • Market cycles. Chinese demand surged around 2015 and then cooled. Commentators in 2021 described the 147.com sale as the lowest reported 3N .com price since 2014, and 509.com resold in 2021 for $106,000 after changing hands at $295,000 in 2018.
  • Renewal costs. A portfolio of hundreds of 6N names costs real money every year. If the sell-through rate is low, renewals can exceed sales.
  • Currency and regulation. Buyers in mainland China face capital controls and domain real-name verification, which can complicate cross-border sales.
  • Unconfirmed prices. Some headline sales, including 360.com, were never officially confirmed. Treat them as indicators, not comparables.

A practical buying checklist

  1. Decide whether you are buying to hold, to flip wholesale, or to sell to an end user.
  2. Avoid 4 everywhere and 0 at the start unless the name is very short.
  3. Pay for pattern, not just "no 4, no 0".
  4. Check comparable sales from the last few years, not the 2015 peak.
  5. Budget at least several years of renewals.
  6. Use escrow or a marketplace transfer service for every purchase.

Questions

What does 4N or 6N mean?

It is shorthand for the number of digits in a numeric domain. A 4N .com such as 8181.com has four digits, and a 6N .com such as 666777.com has six.

Why do numeric domains without 4 or 0 sell for more?

Many buyers are Chinese, and 4 sounds like the word for death. Zero is less clearly unlucky but is often treated as weaker, especially at the start of a name.

Are 6N .com domains a good investment?

Mostly as low-cost inventory traded between investors. There are a million possible names, few end users, and a 2021 review found only 25 sold for more than $1,500 in a year.

Where can I sell a numeric domain?

Common venues are Afternic, GoDaddy Auctions, Sedo, brokers for premium short names, and Chinese marketplaces such as eName and Juming for domestic buyers.
