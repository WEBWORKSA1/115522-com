Lucky and unlucky numbers in 7 cultures

Why 8 sells for a fortune in Hong Kong, why 4 disappears from lift buttons, and why an Indian gift is never a round number.

China

Chinese number luck is mostly about sound. Mandarin and Cantonese have many words that sound alike, so a digit inherits the luck of its homophone. 8 (bā) is close to 发 (fā, prosper) — the Beijing Olympics opened at 8:08 pm on 08-08-08. 6 (liù) suggests smoothness, 9 (jiǔ) long-lasting. 4 (sì) sounds like death and is avoided in phone numbers, floors and prices.

Love numbers are a modern twist: 520 reads as “I love you” and 1314 as “for a lifetime”.

Japan

Japanese wordplay called goroawase reads digits by sound. 4 (shi) sounds like death and 9 (ku) like suffering, so both are avoided — hospitals and hotels often skip rooms 4 and 9. 8 is lucky because the character 八 widens at the bottom like a fan, and 7 is broadly lucky. Wedding gifts use odd amounts so the sum can’t be “split”.

Korea

Korea shares the fear of 4 (sa); lifts often show “F” instead of 4. 7 is lucky, and number slang is everywhere in texting: 1004 (cheon-sa) means “angel”, and 8282 means “hurry”.

India

Vedic numerology links each digit to a planet — 1 Sun, 2 Moon, 3 Jupiter, 4 Rahu, 5 Mercury, 6 Venus, 7 Ketu, 8 Saturn, 9 Mars — and many people pick phone numbers, car plates and wedding dates to match their birth number. Gifts end in 1 (101, 501, 1001). 108 is sacred across Hindu, Buddhist and Jain traditions, and 786 — the Abjad value of the Bismillah — is revered in South Asian Muslim communities.

The Arab world

In the Abjad system, Arabic letters carry numerical values (alif = 1, bā’ = 2 …), which is how 786 came to stand for the Bismillah. In the Gulf, short and repeated numbers are status symbols: plates and phone numbers like 777 7777 and P7 have sold for millions of dirhams at charity auctions.

The West

7 is the world’s favourite number in surveys and the luckiest in Western folklore; 3 is a charm. 13 is unlucky (triskaidekaphobia), Friday the 13th doubly so. In Italy, 17 is unlucky because its Roman numeral XVII rearranges into VIXI, “I have lived”.

Thailand and Vietnam

In Thai, 9 (kâo) sounds like “step forward”, making it the luckiest digit; in Vietnam, 4 is avoided and 9 and 8 are favoured, and lucky money at Tết follows similar rules.

Questions

What is the luckiest number in the world?

There isn’t one. 8 is the most prized digit in Chinese culture, 7 is the West’s favourite, and 9 is lucky in Thailand — while 9 is avoided in Japan.

Why is 4 unlucky in Asia?

In Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese the word for four sounds like the word for death, so buildings, phone numbers and gifts often avoid it.

Why do Indian gifts end in 1?

Amounts like ₹101, ₹501 and ₹1,001 make sure the gift never ends on zero — the extra one stands for continuity and blessing.

What does 520 mean?

In Chinese, 5-2-0 (wǔ èr líng) sounds like “I love you” (wǒ ài nǐ). 20 May has become an unofficial Valentine’s Day.
